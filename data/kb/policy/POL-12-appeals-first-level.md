# Appeals: first level

This policy covers first-level appeals of prior-authorization denials: the deadline, who may file, what to include and how long the decision takes. Appeals are handled by the Appeals Unit, a separate process from the service desk. An appeal is never a service desk ticket.

## How long do I have to file an appeal?
A first-level appeal must be filed within 60 calendar days of the denial date. The denial date is on the **Decision** tab and in the decision letter. The 60 days include weekends and holidays. A peer-to-peer does not pause or extend the appeal deadline, so an office that requests a peer-to-peer should still watch the 60-day date. An appeal filed after 60 calendar days is not accepted, and the service desk cannot extend the deadline.

## Who may file an appeal?
Three parties may file a first-level appeal: the ordering provider, the provider office on the member's behalf, or the member. In the portal, a user with the `appeal.create` permission files it. PROVIDER_NURSE and PROVIDER_OFFICE_MGR users have this permission. The service desk and the assistant cannot file an appeal for anyone. A member who files directly does so through the member channel described in the decision letter, not through the provider portal.

## How do I file an appeal in the portal?
Open the denied request, go to the **Decision** tab and select **Appeal this decision**. State why the decision should be changed and upload the supporting documents. Appeals for denials that are not appealable, such as CR-ND-006, CR-ND-007 and CR-ND-010, are not accepted; the next step for those is in POL-11, Denial reason codes. The appeal appears on the request's **Timeline** tab once it is filed.

## What should an appeal include?
A first-level appeal should include a short statement of why the decision should be reversed, the denial code being appealed, and the documents that address it. For CR-ND-001, include the conservative-therapy history. For CR-ND-002, include clinical notes dated within 60 days. For CR-ND-003, the appeal is a coverage appeal and should cite the plan benefit. Documents follow the standards in POL-09: signed, dated, legible, accepted file types and at most 25 MB per file.

## How long does an appeal decision take?
A standard first-level appeal is decided within 30 days. An expedited appeal is decided within 72 hours. An appeal is expedited when waiting for the standard appeal time could seriously jeopardise the member's life, health or ability to regain maximum function, as attested by the treating clinician. The decision appears on the request's **Decision** tab, and the appeal decision letter is downloaded with **Download letter**.

## Can the service desk expedite or change an appeal?
No. The service desk cannot file, expedite or change an appeal, and the assistant cannot promise an appeal outcome or time. Questions about an appeal in progress go to the Appeals Unit. The desk can help with portal problems that block filing, such as a missing **Appeal this decision** button or a failed upload. Those portal problems are raised as a normal support ticket, not as an appeal.
