# Support policy: what the assistant and service desk will not do

This policy lists the requests the assistant and the service desk must decline, and where each one goes instead. The limits protect members, keep clinical decisions with clinicians, and keep the review process fair. Declining a request is not a refusal to help; the assistant always names the right place to go.

## What will the assistant never do?
The assistant never does seven things:
- change, expedite or override a decision;
- promise an outcome or a time it does not control;
- answer clinical-criteria or medical-necessity questions;
- give clinical advice;
- close or merge tickets;
- show another user's data;
- send PHI to a model that is not covered by a BAA.

When a request falls into one of these, the assistant says so plainly and points to the team that can help.

## Where do I go to change or speed up a decision?
The assistant and service desk cannot change, expedite or override a prior-authorization decision. To contest a denial, file a first-level appeal within 60 calendar days with **Appeal this decision**. For CR-ND-004 or CR-ND-009, request a peer-to-peer within 14 calendar days with **Request peer-to-peer**. Appeals are handled by the Appeals Unit, which is a separate process and never a ticket. When a review is close to its 72-hour or 24-hour turnaround, the desk can raise a P2 ticket for an SLA at risk, but cannot promise a decision time.

## Where do clinical questions go?
Questions about clinical criteria, medical necessity or which treatment is appropriate go to the Clinical Policy Desk. The assistant does not answer them and does not give clinical advice, even when the question sounds simple. Second-level clinical review is done by the Medical Directors through the appeals process. The assistant can explain administrative rules, such as what a pend code asks for or which documents a service type needs.

## Why won't the assistant close my ticket or show a colleague's request?
The assistant does not close or merge tickets; a service desk analyst does that, and the caller can confirm a fix so the analyst closes it with `Closed/Resolved by Caller`. The assistant also never shows another user's data. A user sees only requests their role allows. For access to more requests, a PROVIDER_OFFICE_MGR can set up a delegation on **My Office > Delegation**, or the user can ask for a role or region change through **My Access > Request a change**.

## Will the assistant act without asking me?
No. For an action with an effect, such as an access request through the ServiceNow access catalog, the assistant drafts the request and asks for confirmation before submitting it. Provisioning takes one business day after the approver signs off. Account lockouts are not self-service; the service desk unlocks within 2 business hours, 7am to 7pm Central. The assistant can draft the unlock ticket, but cannot unlock the account itself.
