# Oncology drugs and radiation therapy

This policy sets out what an office submits for oncology drug regimens and radiation therapy under the ONCOLOGY service type. It covers the treatment plan, regimen documentation and urgency. It does not cover which regimen is appropriate; clinical criteria questions go to the Clinical Policy Desk.

## What must an oncology request include?
An ONCOLOGY request needs the oncologist's clinical notes signed and dated within 60 days of submission, a treatment plan, regimen or course details, a diagnosis code that matches the requested service, and the servicing provider's valid NPI. A missing treatment plan is pended with PI-DOC-009. Missing notes are pended with PI-DOC-001, and an unsigned order with PI-DOC-005. A diagnosis code that does not match the requested drug or radiation service is pended with PI-DOC-007.

## What should an oncology treatment plan contain?
The oncology treatment plan is the document that clears a PI-DOC-009 pend. It should state the planned regimen or radiation course, the number of cycles or fractions, the planned start date, the expected end date and the treating facility. It must be signed and dated by the ordering provider. A plan that is clearly a template with blanks is treated as missing. Upload it on step **5 Documents** or through **Respond to Pend**.

## How should drug regimens be documented?
For an oncology drug regimen, list each drug as a separate service line with its units per cycle and the number of cycles requested. The units and dates of service must agree with the treatment plan. When they do not agree, or the units are unclear, the request is pended with PI-DOC-006. Changes to a regimen after approval are handled as described in POL-15, Extensions and modifications.

## How should radiation therapy be documented?
For radiation therapy, the request states the treatment course as units, for example the number of fractions, and the date range of the course. Simulation and planning services that need authorization are entered as their own service lines. The treatment plan supports the request; it does not replace the service lines. Mismatched units or dates lead to a pend with PI-DOC-006.

## Can an oncology request be urgent?
An oncology request can be submitted as URGENT when waiting the standard time could seriously jeopardise the member's life, health or ability to regain maximum function. A treating clinician must tick the **Urgent attestation** checkbox on step **6 Review & Submit**. URGENT requests have a 24-hour decision turnaround; STANDARD requests have 72 hours. The clock pauses while a request is pended, so a complete treatment plan at submission keeps the turnaround short.
