# Behavioral health inpatient and IOP

This policy sets out what an office or facility submits for behavioral health inpatient stays and intensive outpatient programs (IOP) under the BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH service type. It covers the treatment plan and the concurrent review cadence. Questions about level-of-care criteria go to the Clinical Policy Desk.

## What must a behavioral health request include?
A BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH request needs current clinical notes signed and dated within 60 days of submission, a treatment plan, the requested level of care (inpatient or IOP), the number of days or sessions requested, a diagnosis code that matches the service, and the servicing facility's valid NPI. A missing treatment plan is pended with PI-DOC-009. Missing notes are pended with PI-DOC-001.

## What should a behavioral health treatment plan contain?
The behavioral health treatment plan clears a PI-DOC-009 pend. It should state the treatment goals, the planned interventions, the planned length of stay or program length, the discharge or step-down plan, and the treating clinician. It must be signed and dated. For IOP, the plan also states the number of sessions per week. Upload the plan on step **5 Documents** or through **Respond to Pend**.

## What is concurrent review for behavioral health?
Concurrent review is the review of a continuing stay or program while it is in progress. The first approval covers a set number of days or sessions. Before those run out, the facility submits an update so the reviewer can decide on further days. For inpatient stays, updates are due every 7 days. For IOP, updates are due every 14 days. Each update is a new request that references the PA number and includes current notes and the updated treatment plan.

## What happens if a concurrent review update is late?
If a concurrent review update for a behavioral health stay is submitted after the approved days run out, the days between the end of the approval and the submission are not covered by the existing authorization. Submit updates before the approved period ends. A late update is reviewed like any other request, and the review SLA starts when it is submitted.

## Can a behavioral health request be urgent?
A behavioral health request can be submitted as URGENT when waiting the standard time could seriously jeopardise the member's life, health or ability to regain maximum function. A treating clinician must tick the **Urgent attestation** checkbox on step **6 Review & Submit**. URGENT requests are decided within 24 hours and STANDARD requests within 72 hours. An emergency admission that happened before a request could be filed is handled as a retro authorization within 14 calendar days of the service date.
